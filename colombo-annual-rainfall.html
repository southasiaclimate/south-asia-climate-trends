Colombo Annual Rainfall: 30 Years of Trend Data (1996–2025)

Published August 17, 2026 · Colombo, Sri Lanka
Quick answer: Colombo's annual rainfall typically falls between 2,200mm and 3,000mm, with a 30-year ERA5 trend line rising from around 2,400mm in the mid-1990s to roughly 2,900mm by 2025 — an increase of approximately +170mm per decade. The wettest years on record, 2023 and 2024, both topped 3,300mm.

Most answers to "how much rain does Colombo get" cite a single long-term average — typically somewhere between 2,400mm and 2,608mm, based on multi-decade climatological normals. That figure is accurate as a baseline, but it answers a different question than the one most people actually want to know: is Colombo's rainfall changing, and if so, how?

This report answers that second question directly, using year-by-year ERA5/ERA5-Land reanalysis data covering 1996–2025, rather than a single static average. It's a companion piece to our full Colombo climate trend report, which also covers temperature and seasonal breakdowns.

How Much Does It Rain in Colombo Each Year?

Annual rainfall totals sit in the 2,200–3,000mm range for nearly the entire 30-year record — roughly 4 times Delhi's typical annual total and well above even Dhaka's substantial rainfall. Colombo is, by a wide margin, the wettest of the eight South Asian cities covered in this series, a reflection of its position on a tropical island exposed to both the southwest and northeast monsoons.

Is Colombo's Rainfall Increasing?

Yes — and the increase shows up consistently across the record, not just in a handful of outlier years. The trend line climbs steadily from around 2,400mm in the mid-1990s to roughly 2,900mm by 2025.

Key Figure: Annual Precipitation Trend

Approximately +170mm per decade

This puts Colombo's rainfall trajectory in line with the pattern found in Karachi and Delhi — both trending wetter — rather than Dhaka, which shows a declining rainfall trend despite warming faster overall. Combined with an already-high baseline, Colombo's rising trend reinforces its position as the consistently wettest city in this dataset.

Monsoon season delivers the single largest seasonal total, but it's pre-monsoon (March–May) that shows the steepest rainfall trend of any season at roughly +50mm per decade, followed closely by post-monsoon's +40mm per decade — the latter still driven substantially by a dramatic outlier year around 2006, which spiked to nearly 1,450mm, roughly triple a typical year for that season. Monsoon itself is the flattest of the four seasonal rainfall trends, at around +17mm per decade, even though it delivers the largest absolute totals.

Why these numbers might differ slightly from other sources: most published "average annual rainfall" figures for Colombo (typically 2,400–2,608mm) are long-term climatological normals — often averaged over 30–45+ years of station data ending around the present. This report instead tracks the year-by-year trend across 1996–2025 using ERA5/ERA5-Land reanalysis, which is why we report a range and a trend line rather than a single fixed average. Both approaches are valid; they simply answer different questions — "what's typical" versus "what's changing."

Data Source & Methodology

All figures in this report are derived from the Open-Meteo Historical Weather API, built on ERA5 and ERA5-Land reanalysis data produced by the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts (ECMWF) through the Copernicus Climate Change Service, covering January 1995 through 2025. Trend rates are estimated by reading the linear regression trend line plotted on the annual precipitation chart across the full study period. Seasonal boundaries follow the standard South Asian meteorological convention: Winter (December–February), Pre-Monsoon/Hot (March–May), Monsoon (June–September), and Post-Monsoon (October–November).

Frequently Asked Questions

How much rain does Colombo get per year?

Based on 1996–2025 ERA5 reanalysis data, Colombo's annual rainfall typically falls in the 2,200–3,000mm range, with the trend line rising from around 2,400mm in the mid-1990s to roughly 2,900mm by 2025.

Is Colombo's rainfall increasing?

Yes. The 30-year ERA5 record shows an increasing trend of approximately +170mm per decade at the annual level, with every individual season also showing a positive precipitation trend.

What is the wettest year on record for Colombo?

The wettest years in the 1996–2025 ERA5 record are 2023 and 2024, both exceeding 3,300mm of annual rainfall — well above the long-term trend line.

Which season has the most rain in Colombo?

The monsoon season (June–September) delivers Colombo's largest single seasonal total, typically 1,000–1,050mm, though pre-monsoon (March–May) shows the steepest rainfall trend of any season over the 30-year record.

How does Colombo's rainfall compare to other South Asian cities?

Colombo is the wettest of the eight South Asian cities studied in this series, receiving roughly 4–8 times Karachi's typical annual rainfall and substantially more than Delhi, Dhaka, or Mumbai.
