Delhi Annual Rainfall: 30 Years of Trend Data (1996–2025)

Published September 8, 2026 · Delhi, India
Quick answer: Delhi's annual rainfall typically falls between 450mm and 950mm, with a 30-year trend line showing a modest increase (~+35mm per decade). But 2024 broke that pattern entirely — a record ~1,200mm, well clear of any other year on record. Averaging the most recent decade (2016–2025) puts typical annual rainfall at roughly 740mm, though that figure is itself pulled upward by 2024's outlier year.

Delhi's rainfall story has two layers: a real but modest long-term increase, and a much more dramatic single-year event in 2024 that dominates recent memory. This report separates the two, using year-by-year ERA5/ERA5-Land reanalysis data covering 1996–2025 — a companion to our full Delhi climate trend report and extreme heat days report.

How Much Does It Rain in Delhi Each Year?

Most years fall in a fairly consistent 450–950mm band. 2024 is the clear exception — at roughly 1,200mm, it stands well apart from every other year in the 30-year record, driven by an exceptionally heavy monsoon season.

What Was the Average Rainfall in Delhi Over the Last 10 Years?

Averaging 2016–2025 gives approximately 740mm — but this figure should be read carefully. Several years in that same decade (2016, 2017, 2018, 2022) sat closer to 585–610mm, while 2024's record year alone pulls the ten-year average up substantially. Excluding 2024, the more typical decade average would land closer to 660–680mm.

Is Delhi's Rainfall Increasing?

Key Figure: Annual Precipitation Trend

Approximately +35mm per decade

A real but modest increase — slower than Karachi (+88.7mm/decade) or Islamabad (+60mm/decade), and like both of those cities, Delhi's trend is disproportionately shaped by a small number of extreme years rather than a broad year-over-year climb.

Key Insight

Delhi's fastest-warming season isn't getting wetter

Post-monsoon (October–November) is Delhi's fastest-warming season — approximately +0.37°C per decade, well ahead of every other season, rising from a trend-line baseline near 22.4°C in the mid-1990s to about 23.5°C by 2025. But post-monsoon rainfall over the same 30 years shows no comparable increase — if anything, a slight decline (~−3mm/decade). The same pattern shows up in Islamabad, where the fastest-warming season (winter) also has declining, not rising, rainfall. In both cities, the season warming fastest and the season getting wetter fastest turn out to be different seasons entirely.

A note on 2024's monsoon: the record annual total traces almost entirely back to an exceptional monsoon season (~1,090mm, itself a 30-year record). This is consistent with the pattern seen in Karachi's 2022 — a single extreme monsoon year doing most of the work behind an otherwise modest long-term trend.

Data Source & Methodology

All figures in this report are derived from the Open-Meteo Historical Weather API, built on ERA5 and ERA5-Land reanalysis data produced by the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts (ECMWF) through the Copernicus Climate Change Service, covering January 1996 through 2025. Trend rates are estimated by reading the linear regression trend line plotted on each chart across the full study period. Seasonal boundaries follow the standard South Asian meteorological convention: Winter (December–February), Pre-Monsoon/Hot (March–May), Monsoon (June–September), and Post-Monsoon (October–November).

Frequently Asked Questions

How much rain does Delhi get per year?

Based on 1996–2025 ERA5 reanalysis data, Delhi's annual rainfall typically falls between 450mm and 950mm, with 2024 a dramatic outlier at roughly 1,200mm — the wettest year on record by a wide margin.

What was the average rainfall in Delhi over the last 10 years?

Averaging 2016–2025, Delhi's annual rainfall was approximately 740mm — pulled upward significantly by 2024's record ~1,200mm year, which is nearly double several other years in the same decade.

Is Delhi's rainfall increasing?

Modestly, at roughly +35mm per decade at the annual level, though the trend is heavily influenced by outlier years like 2024 rather than a broad, steady climb across most years.

Is Delhi's fastest-warming season also getting wetter?

No. Post-monsoon (October–November) is Delhi's fastest-warming season at approximately +0.37°C per decade, but post-monsoon rainfall is flat to slightly declining over the same 30 years — the two trends move independently.

What is the wettest year on record for Delhi?

2024, at approximately 1,200mm — substantially higher than any other year in the 1996–2025 ERA5 record, driven by an exceptionally heavy monsoon season.
