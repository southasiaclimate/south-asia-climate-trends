South Asia's Climate Records: Hottest, Coldest, Wettest, and Driest Years, City by City

Published September 11, 2026 · Regional Reference
Quick summary: Across 30 years of ERA5 reanalysis data spanning 9 South Asian cities and locations, several records stand out sharply: Delhi's 2024 monsoon nearly doubled any other year on record; Karachi's 2022 rainfall was roughly 27 times its 1990s baseline; and a striking number of temperature records — in Mumbai, Dhaka, and elsewhere — were set within just the last two years of the entire 30-year study period.

This report pulls together every hottest, coldest, wettest, and driest year already documented across our full city-by-city series, in one reference page. Every figure below is sourced from the individual full reports — follow the links throughout for complete seasonal breakdowns and methodology.

Hottest Years

Mumbai — 2025: 27.3°C annual mean
The warmest year on record, edging out 2024 (27.1°C) — part of a sharp recent acceleration that saw Mumbai's annual mean jump nearly a full degree between 2022 and 2025.
Dhaka — 2025: ~26.5°C annual mean
The highest point in Dhaka's entire 30-year record, part of the steadiest, most consistent warming climb of any city in this series.
Delhi — 2009 & 2025 (tied): ~25.3°C annual mean
Notably, neither of these years was also Delhi's record year for extreme heat days — that record belongs to 2010, a genuinely different kind of hot year.
Islamabad — 2022: 22.6°C annual mean
Part of a dramatic post-2015 step-up in Islamabad's annual temperatures, with winter specifically warming faster than any other season anywhere in this series.

Coldest Years

Kathmandu — 1997: 17.15°C annual mean
The coldest point in Kathmandu's record — fitting, given Kathmandu is the only location in this series without a clear warming trend.
Delhi — 1997: 23.0°C annual mean
By a wide margin the coldest year in Delhi's 30-year record.
Islamabad — 1995 (winter): 8.9°C seasonal mean
The coldest winter on record — remarkable given the same season now holds Islamabad's fastest-warming trend of any season in this entire series, nearly 5°C warmer by 2025.
Lahore — 2024 (winter, last-decade record): ~12.05°C seasonal mean
The coldest winter of the past 10 years, narrowly below 2019 — notable as an exception within Lahore's broader winter warming trend.

Wettest Years

Delhi — 2024: ~1,200mm annual
Nearly double most other years in the record, driven by an exceptional ~1,090mm monsoon — itself a 30-year record for that season alone.
Karachi — 2022: ~830mm annual
The most dramatic relative departure of any record in this series — roughly 27 times Karachi's 1990s trend-line baseline, coinciding with Pakistan's catastrophic 2022 monsoon flooding.
Mumbai — 2020: ~2,750mm annual
Part of a much steadier, broader-based rainfall increase than Delhi's or Karachi's outlier-driven trends — see our Mumbai vs. Karachi comparison for why that distinction matters.
Colombo — 2023 & 2024: Both above 3,300mm
Consistent with Colombo's position as the wettest city in this entire series on a typical-year basis — see our Colombo annual rainfall report.
Islamabad — 2020: ~1,440mm annual
The clear outlier in Islamabad's record, well above any other year.

Driest Years

Lahore — 2021: ~385mm annual
The driest year in Lahore's record, against a rainfall trend that's essentially flat (~+10mm/decade) over 30 years — see our Lahore annual rainfall report.
Islamabad — 2000 & 2009: ~615–670mm annual
Both roughly half the record year (2020)'s total.
Delhi — 2006 & 2013: ~460mm annual
Tied for the driest years in Delhi's record.
Rasuwagadhi — 2025 (monsoon): ~620mm
By far the lowest monsoon total on record for Rasuwagadhi, part of the steepest rainfall decline documented anywhere in this series (~−367mm/decade annually).

Delhi's 2010 record is genuinely striking on its own terms: as our extreme heat report found, 2010 wasn't even Delhi's hottest year by annual average — proof that "most extreme heat days" and "hottest year overall" can be entirely different records.

A pattern worth noting: a disproportionate number of the records above — Mumbai's, Dhaka's, and Delhi's hottest years, several rainfall records — were set within just the final two to three years of this 30-year dataset (2023–2025). Whether this reflects accelerating climate trends, short-term natural variability, or both, is a question best answered as more years of data accumulate — but it's a pattern consistent across multiple, independently analyzed cities.

Data Source & Methodology

All records in this report are compiled from our individual city reports, each built on the Open-Meteo Historical Weather API using ERA5 and ERA5-Land reanalysis data from the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts (ECMWF) via the Copernicus Climate Change Service, covering January 1995/96 through 2025 (Rasuwagadhi's underlying dataset spans a slightly longer window). Individual record values should be read alongside each city's full report for seasonal context and methodology notes — this page is a reference index, not a substitute for the underlying analysis.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which South Asian city had the wettest year on record?

Mumbai's 2020 (~2,750mm) and Islamabad's 2020 (~1,440mm) both stand out, but in relative terms, Karachi's 2022 (~830mm, roughly 27 times its 1990s baseline) and Delhi's 2024 (~1,200mm, nearly double any other year) represent the most dramatic single-year departures from normal across this 9-location series.

Which South Asian city had the hottest year on record?

Mumbai's 2025 (27.3°C annual mean) and Dhaka's 2025 (~26.5°C) both set records, alongside Islamabad's 2022 (22.6°C). Several cities in this series recorded their hottest year on record within the final two years of the 30-year study period.

Which South Asian city had the coldest year on record?

Kathmandu's 1997 (17.15°C annual mean) and Delhi's 1997 (23.0°C) both stand out as record cold years, alongside Islamabad's coldest winter on record, also 1995 (8.9°C seasonal mean).

Which South Asian city had the driest year on record?

Lahore's 2021 (~385mm) and Islamabad's shared low years around 2000 and 2009 (~615–670mm) stand out as the most pronounced dry-year records in this series.

What is the most extreme single-year climate record in this entire series?

Karachi's 2022 annual rainfall (~830mm against a 1990s baseline near 30mm) is arguably the most extreme relative departure in the dataset — roughly 27 times the trend-line baseline for that era, coinciding with Pakistan's catastrophic 2022 monsoon floods.
