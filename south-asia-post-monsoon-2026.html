South Asia's Post-Monsoon 2026: Monsoon Exit, Cyclone Outlook, and 30 Years of Data

Published October 27, 2026 · Regional
This is a historical-data and seasonal-context report, not a real-time forecast or warning. For live cyclone tracking and warnings, use your national meteorological service: IMD (India), PMD (Pakistan), BMD (Bangladesh), or the Department of Meteorology, Sri Lanka.
Quick summary: India's southwest monsoon withdrew from the whole country on October 15, 2026, exactly on the normal date. The open question is the October-December cyclone season: ISRO's September seasonal outlook describes three North Indian Ocean cyclones as possible, with overall activity well above the long-term mean. That is an estimate of a busier-than-average season, not a prediction of any particular storm.

This follows our 2026 monsoon update, which tracked the season through August. Now that the monsoon has gone, this report covers how it ended, what the post-monsoon cyclone outlook does and does not say, and what 30 years of data in this series shows for the same season.

How the 2026 Monsoon Ended

IMD declared complete withdrawal of the southwest monsoon from the entire country on Thursday, October 15, 2026, matching the normal climatological date. For context, withdrawal came on October 16 in 2025 (+1 day), October 19 in 2023 (+4 days) and October 23 in 2022 (+8 days). The exit itself was unremarkable. The notable weather came just before it: deep depression BOB 06 (September 21-27) was linked to heavy rain and flooding, with 88 deaths reported across Uttar Pradesh, Andhra Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Odisha and Nepal, according to the compiled season record.

The Cyclone Outlook: What Is and Is Not Established

October-December is one of two main North Indian Ocean cyclone windows (the other is April-June). The ISRO Space Applications Centre outlook, issued in September 2026, calls three tropical cyclones a "possibility" and gives a forecast power dissipation index of about 13.1 × 106 knots3, against a 1982-2025 mean of about 7.15 × 106 knots3. PDI combines storm frequency, intensity and lifetime, so a higher value points to a more active season.

The season so far, per the compiled record, has had six systems (three deep depressions) and no cyclonic storm or stronger, against a typical three to four cyclonic storms per season. Seasonal outlooks give broad context months ahead; storm-specific forecasts only become meaningful days ahead and come from the national agencies.

Frequently Asked Questions

Did the 2026 monsoon withdraw late?

No. IMD declared complete withdrawal of the southwest monsoon from the whole country on October 15, 2026, matching the normal date (zero days deviation). Withdrawal was 1 day late in 2025 and 8 days late in 2022.

Is a major cyclone confirmed to hit South Asia in late 2026?

No. ISRO's Space Applications Centre seasonal outlook, issued in September 2026, describes three tropical cyclones over the North Indian Ocean in October-December as a possibility, with a power dissipation index (about 13.1 × 106 knots3) well above the 1982-2025 mean (about 7.15 × 106). That is a seasonal activity estimate, not a forecast of any specific storm, track, or landfall.

Why is October to December a cyclone season?

The North Indian Ocean has two cyclone windows each year, April-June and October-December. After the monsoon retreats, warm sea surfaces and changing winds over the Bay of Bengal and Arabian Sea favor storm formation.

What does 30 years of data show about post-monsoon extremes?

Mumbai's 2019 post-monsoon total (about 290 mm) and Colombo's 2006 total (about 1,450 mm) stand far above their own 30-year baselines. Reanalysis totals alone cannot tie individual years to specific storms, but both are consistent with this being a season where a single extreme event can dominate.

Where can I get official cyclone warnings?

Use your national meteorological service: IMD (India), PMD (Pakistan), BMD (Bangladesh), or the Department of Meteorology (Sri Lanka). This site provides historical context only.
