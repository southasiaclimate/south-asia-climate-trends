Mumbai vs. Karachi: Two Coastal Cities, Two Opposite Rainfall Stories

Published September 10, 2026 · Regional Comparison
Quick answer: Both cities sit on the Arabian Sea, but that's where the similarity in their rainfall stories ends. Mumbai's precipitation trend (~+300mm/decade) is the steepest of any city in this entire series — a broad, sustained monsoon intensification. Karachi's rise (+88.7mm/decade) is real but tells a completely different kind of story: driven almost entirely by a handful of extreme years, especially 2022, rather than a steady climb.

Mumbai and Karachi are both major Arabian Sea port cities, both economic engines for their countries, both shaped by coastal geography. It would be reasonable to expect their climate trends to look broadly similar. They don't. Using the same 30-year ERA5/ERA5-Land reanalysis methodology applied throughout this site, this report puts them side by side, drawing on our full Mumbai climate trend report and Karachi climate trend report.

Two Coastal Cities, Same Sea, Very Different Rain

"Coastal" turns out to explain far less than you'd expect. Both cities benefit from ocean moderation on temperature extremes — neither sees much extreme heat compared to inland cities like Delhi or Lahore. But rainfall depends on something else entirely: where each city sits relative to the monsoon's actual moisture corridor. Mumbai sits directly in the path of the core southwest monsoon flow moving up India's west coast, backed by the Western Ghats. Karachi sits near the arid western edge of the monsoon's typical reach, where rainfall has always been naturally marginal, episodic, and desert-adjacent — a fundamentally drier baseline climate that a rising trend doesn't erase.

The Rainfall Character Difference

This is the real story here — not just that Mumbai's numbers are bigger, but that the two trends are different kinds of trends entirely.

  • Mumbai's increase is broad and sustained. Annual rainfall climbs from a trend-line baseline near 1,550mm in the mid-1990s to roughly 2,450mm by 2025 — a steady, largely consistent climb. The monsoon alone (which supplies ~85% of Mumbai's annual rain) shows a precipitation trend of approximately +233mm/decade, rising from ~1,500mm to ~2,200mm. The wettest year on record, 2020 (~2,750mm annual, ~2,620mm monsoon), is a strong year but not wildly disconnected from the surrounding trend.
  • Karachi's increase is a small-number-of-years story. For most of 1995–2015, Karachi's annual rainfall sat in a modest 50–220mm range. Then, from around 2018 onward, isolated extreme years reshaped the trend line entirely: ~540mm around 2020, and a record ~830mm in 2022 alone — nearly four times typical totals from a decade earlier. Remove 2022, and Karachi's rainfall story looks far less dramatic.
What this means practically: Mumbai's flood risk is now elevated in a typical year, not just an exceptional one — infrastructure planning has to assume the new, higher baseline is normal. Karachi's risk is different: most years remain genuinely dry and arid, but drainage infrastructure built for that dry norm is being tested by rare, extreme events its historical design never anticipated. Same direction of change, very different planning problem.

The Temperature Story

Mumbai is warming roughly twice as fast as Karachi — but the more interesting detail is when. Mumbai's trend line understates the most recent years dramatically: annual mean temperature jumped from about 26.4°C in 2022 to 27.3°C in 2025, nearly a full degree in a span that would normally take three to four decades at the underlying rate. Winter specifically — historically Mumbai's most stable season — has "abruptly caught fire since 2023," rising roughly 2.2°C in just three years. Karachi shows no comparable recent acceleration; our full Karachi report notes its warming pace is "broadly consistent with the global average land-surface warming trend," suggesting a wider regional signal rather than a strong local driver.

Data Source & Methodology

All figures in this report are drawn from our full Mumbai climate trend report and Karachi climate trend report, both built on the Open-Meteo Historical Weather API using ERA5 and ERA5-Land reanalysis data from the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts (ECMWF) via the Copernicus Climate Change Service, covering January 1995/96 through 2025. Trend rates are estimated by reading the linear regression trend line plotted on each city's charts. Seasonal boundaries follow the standard South Asian meteorological convention: Winter (December–February), Pre-Monsoon/Hot (March–May), Monsoon (June–September), and Post-Monsoon (October–November).

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do Mumbai and Karachi have such different rainfall trends if they're both coastal?

Being coastal moderates temperature for both cities similarly, but rainfall depends on monsoon exposure, not just proximity to the sea. Mumbai sits directly in the path of the core southwest monsoon flow up India's west coast, while Karachi sits near the arid western edge of the monsoon's typical reach, where rainfall is naturally more marginal and episodic.

Which city has a steeper rainfall trend, Mumbai or Karachi?

Mumbai, by a wide margin. Mumbai's annual precipitation trend is approximately +300mm per decade, the steepest of any city in this series, driven by a broad, sustained monsoon intensification. Karachi's trend (+88.7mm per decade) is real but driven almost entirely by a small number of extreme years, especially 2022.

Is Mumbai or Karachi warming faster?

Mumbai, at approximately +0.25°C per decade versus Karachi's +0.13°C per decade — roughly double. Mumbai's warming has also accelerated sharply since 2023, particularly in winter, while Karachi's trend has stayed closer to the global average land-warming pace throughout the record.

Does Mumbai or Karachi see more extreme heat?

Both are low compared to inland cities in this series, reflecting coastal moderation. Mumbai sits at essentially zero extreme-heat days across the 30-year record with one flagged exception around 2023–24. Karachi also stays low overall but shows occasional clustered spikes of 3–5 days in certain years rather than Mumbai's near-total absence.

What does a rising rainfall trend mean differently for Mumbai versus Karachi?

For Mumbai, a steady, broad-based increase means consistently higher flood risk most years, compounding with rapid urban warming. For Karachi, an outlier-driven trend means most years remain dry and arid as normal, but the rare extreme years — like 2022 — can overwhelm infrastructure built for a much drier typical climate.
