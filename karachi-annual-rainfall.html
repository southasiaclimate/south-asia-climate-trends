Karachi Annual Rainfall: 30 Years of Trend Data (1996–2025)

Published September 1, 2026 · Karachi, Sindh, Pakistan
Quick answer: Karachi's annual rainfall typically falls between 200mm and 800mm, with a 30-year ERA5 trend line rising from around 30mm in the mid-1990s to roughly 290mm by 2025 — an increase of approximately +88.7mm per decade. But that trend hides an important nuance: most individual years are far drier than the trend line suggests, and the rise is driven largely by a handful of extreme monsoon years — above all 2022, Karachi's wettest year on record at roughly 830mm.

Most answers to "how much rain does Karachi get" cite a single long-term average, usually somewhere in the 150–250mm range based on climatological normals. That figure understates how dramatically variable Karachi's rainfall actually is year to year — and it says nothing about direction of change. This report uses year-by-year ERA5/ERA5-Land reanalysis data covering 1996–2025 to answer both questions directly. It's a companion piece to our full Karachi climate trend report, which also covers temperature and seasonal breakdowns.

How Much Does It Rain in Karachi Each Year?

Karachi is one of the driest major cities in this series on a typical-year basis. Most years fall well under 250mm — often closer to 50–150mm — but the record is punctuated by a small number of dramatically wetter years that pull the long-term trend line upward. This is a fundamentally different shape than Colombo's rainfall pattern, which rises fairly steadily; Karachi's rise is lumpy and outlier-driven.

Is Karachi's Rainfall Increasing?

Yes — the trend line is unambiguous, climbing from around 30mm in the mid-1990s to roughly 290mm by 2025. But this is a case where the trend rate alone can mislead: it's being driven overwhelmingly by extreme wet years rather than a broad-based year-over-year increase.

Key Figure: Annual Precipitation Trend

Approximately +88.7mm per decade

Compare this to Delhi (~+35mm/decade, a steadier climb) or Colombo (~+170mm/decade, from a much higher and more consistent baseline). Karachi's number is respectable in isolation, but the underlying pattern — mostly-dry years punctuated by occasional extreme monsoon events — is arguably the more important story than the trend rate itself.

Data Source & Methodology

All figures in this report are derived from the Open-Meteo Historical Weather API, built on ERA5 and ERA5-Land reanalysis data produced by the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts (ECMWF) through the Copernicus Climate Change Service, covering January 1996 through 2025. Trend rates are estimated by reading the linear regression trend line plotted on the annual precipitation chart across the full study period. Seasonal boundaries follow the standard South Asian meteorological convention: Winter (December–February), Pre-Monsoon/Hot (March–May), Monsoon (June–September), and Post-Monsoon (October–November).

Because Karachi's rainfall is so heavily skewed by a small number of extreme years, a linear trend rate should be read as a general direction rather than a reliable predictor of any single upcoming year's totals — median rainfall in a typical year remains well below the trend line's current value.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much rain does Karachi get per year?

Based on 1996–2025 ERA5 reanalysis data, Karachi's annual rainfall typically falls in the 200–800mm range, though most individual years land well under 250mm — the average is pulled up by a small number of extreme monsoon years.

Is Karachi's rainfall increasing?

Yes. The 30-year ERA5 record shows an increasing trend of approximately +88.7mm per decade at the annual level, though the increase is driven mainly by a handful of extreme years rather than a steady year-over-year climb.

What is the wettest year on record for Karachi?

2022 is the wettest year in the 1996–2025 ERA5 record by a wide margin, at roughly 830mm — nearly triple a typical year — coinciding with Pakistan's catastrophic 2022 monsoon floods. 2019 is the second-wettest year on record, at roughly 540mm.

Which season has the most rain in Karachi?

The monsoon season (June–September) delivers the large majority of Karachi's annual rainfall and drives nearly all of its rising trend; pre-monsoon, post-monsoon, and winter each contribute only marginal, near-flat totals.

How does Karachi's rainfall compare to other South Asian cities?

Karachi is one of the driest cities in this series on a typical-year basis, but its rainfall trend (+88.7mm/decade) is rising faster than Delhi's and considerably faster than Dhaka's declining trend — though nowhere near Mumbai's or Colombo's much higher totals and trends.
